How Americans Get an Italian Driver's License

Your US license stops counting after a year in Italy and cannot be converted. Every step to an Italian license, with the cost and the timing.

Getting a Category B license in Italy takes several stages, and this app prepares you for one of them, the theory exam. You can go through a driving school (autoscuola), which files your paperwork and books your exams, or apply as a private candidate (privatista) and do that yourself. Most immigrants pick a school and study the theory here instead of in the school's Italian class, since attending a theory course is not required.

Either way, you must be at least 18 and resident in Italy. A non-EU citizen must also show a valid residence permit (permesso di soggiorno).

Through a driving school

Step 1: Study the theory

Work through the official questions in this app, in English and then in Italian, before anything else. It usually takes longer than 3 months, and the clocks in the later steps are short: the medical certificate is valid for 3 months, and the filed application gives you 6 months for the theory exam. Once you can answer all the questions correctly in Italian without help in English, you should be ready to book the two medical visits described in the steps below. Certainly, by the time the app suggests that you take mock exams, you should book them.

Step 2: Enroll

Choose an authorized driving school and sign up. The school tells you which documents and medical papers to bring, and it usually takes your photo for the application. There is no single directory of schools, because they are licensed locally, so search your area and confirm the school is authorized. A school can be an expensive choice of little value if it offers no support to a student whose Italian is limited, so shop around: ask whether the textbook comes in English as well as Italian, and whether an instructor speaks English well enough to answer your questions. Most of a school's clients are teenagers, and an older American may feel a little out of place in that setting, which is one more reason to visit before signing up.

Step 3: Get the medical-history note

Your own doctor (medico di base) writes the certificato anamnestico from the medical records they hold, usually without examining you. Your doctor charges for this note, because the national health service does not cover it. Expect to pay about €50. Read the note before you hand it in. Get it only when you are ready to file within 3 months, since the medical certificate it leads to must be under 3 months old when the application is filed.

What the note asks. The note has one line for each of these conditions, in this order, and your doctor answers yes or no on each.

  • Heart and circulation (apparato cardio-circolatorio)
  • Diabetes (diabete mellito)
  • Endocrine system (sistema endocrino)
  • Nervous system (apparato neurologico)
  • Psychiatric conditions (patologie psichiche)
  • Epilepsy (epilessia)
  • Dependence on alcohol, drugs or psychoactive substances (condizioni di dipendenza da alcol/sostanze stupefacenti e psicotrope)
  • Urinary and genital system, severe kidney failure (apparato urogenitale, insufficienza renale grave)
  • Blood and blood-forming organs, meaning hemophilia, or a lymphoma or leukemia under treatment (sangue ed organi emopoietici)
  • Bones and joints, serious anatomical or functional impairment (apparato osteoarticolare)
  • Sense organs, serious eye disease that is getting worse (organi di senso)

A yes in any of these categories can delay your license. The authorized doctor in the next step reviews every yes, may ask for a specialist's report first, and may send you to the local medical commission, where the wait for a visit can run to several months. If a line does not match your condition, ask your doctor about it before the note is used.

Step 4: Pass the medical

You book the visit with a Ministry-authorized doctor through the school, usually on its own premises. Bring the certificato anamnestico, since the doctor cannot issue the certificate without it. This doctor, not your own, checks your eyesight, reviews the note, and issues the medical certificate.

If the note has a yes. This doctor decides whether to certify you or to send you to the local medical commission (Commissione Medica Locale). A yes does not by itself rule out a license, but expect it to take longer. The commission judges epilepsy unless a neurologist certifies it cured, past dependence on alcohol, regular use of medicines that affect driving, serious psychiatric and neurological conditions, kidney failure that needs dialysis or a transplant, and physical impairments. A heart condition needs a report from a cardiologist at a public facility, and diabetes a report from a diabetologist. With that report this doctor may certify you, or may send you to the commission. Any other doubt this doctor has about your fitness to drive also sends you there. For any condition marked on the note, bring a recent report from your specialist to this visit.

Step 5: The school files your application

It submits the TT2112 form to your provincial Motorizzazione office with your documents, the medical certificate and the fee receipts. The medical certificate must be under 3 months old when the application is filed. From then on you have 6 months and 2 attempts to pass the theory exam, and at least a month must pass between a failed attempt and the next.

Step 6: Pass the theory exam

It has 30 true/false questions in 20 minutes, and you may get no more than 3 wrong. It is offered in Italian, with French and German versions, and never in English. No interpreter or dictionary is allowed in the room. It is held in computerized rooms at the Motorizzazione, not at the school. This is what this app prepares you for.

The school decides when to put you forward, and most will not do it until they have seen a good average on their own practice sheets, taken in the school's own app. Most schools use a publisher's textbook and practice app, and the school reads your averages there. Your work here is not visible to them, so plan on running their sheets as well once your study is done. This is the school's own condition and not a Ministry rule. Ask early what average it wants and over how many sheets, and how far ahead the exam dates are running.

On the day. This is what candidates commonly meet, though offices differ. Your phone, switched off, goes into a locker with your coat and hat, and nothing comes into the room with you. A camera checks your face at the entrance and again at your desk, against the photo on your application. The staff explain the procedure in Italian only. Before the exam starts, the screen asks you to accept two statements, so ask your school beforehand what they say. You must accept them to begin. The exam screen has the same layout as the mock exam in this app, and you can go back over your answers before ending it. The result may not appear on the screen at the end, and it can reach you later through the school.

Step 7: Get your foglio rosa and take the mandatory lessons

Passing the theory exam earns the foglio rosa, a learner permit valid 12 months. You must complete 6 hours of certified lessons at the school, in three blocks of 2 hours: on the autostrada or a main extra-urban road, on a secondary extra-urban road or an urban arterial road, and at night. A lesson lasts at most 2 hours a day, and the driving test can be booked only once all 6 hours are certified. A decree of November 2025 raises this to 8 hours in four modules for a foglio rosa issued once it takes effect, so ask your school which rule applies to you. After the school certifies them, you may practice with a companion who sits beside you, is no older than 65, and has held a B license for at least 10 years. The car must display a "P" plate.

Step 8: Pass the driving test

The school books it and provides the car, which must have dual controls. You may take it from 1 month after the foglio rosa, with up to 3 attempts within its 12 months and at least a month between a failed attempt and the next. A Motorizzazione examiner checks that you prepare the car for safe driving, watches two maneuvers, one of them in reverse, and rides with you for at least 25 minutes in normal traffic. Taking the test on an automatic is allowed, but the license then carries code 78 and is valid only for automatics.

Step 9: Receive your license

A B license covers cars and vans up to 3.5 t with up to 9 seats, a light trailer up to 750 kg, and, in Italy only, motorcycles up to 125 cc and 11 kW. It is valid 10 years until age 50, then 5 years to 70, 3 years to 80, and 2 years after that. As a new driver (neopatentato) you face extra limits for the first three years, such as reduced vehicle power and a stricter alcohol limit.

Cost

There is no single fee. The certificato anamnestico costs about €50, and the medical certificate carries a €16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) on top of the authorized doctor's fee. The school's enrollment and paperwork fee usually runs €150–300, more in big cities, and it normally includes the Ministry fees. Lessons are usually sold in 30-minute units of about €30 each, less in packages, so the 6 mandatory hours come to roughly €300–400. All in, getting the license through a school typically costs €800–1,500. Expect a small deposit when exams are booked, forfeited for an unjustified absence, and a 24-hour cancellation rule on lessons.

You book a Ministry-authorized doctor yourself. That is the ASL medical office, booked through the CUP, or an authorized doctor who sees candidates at a driving school or a vehicle-paperwork agency (agenzia di pratiche auto). Bring the certificato anamnestico, which on its own does not count as the medical certificate. Step 4: Pay the fees

Pay on pagoPA, through the Portale dell'Automobilista, in your own name. Enrolling is tariff N067, €26.40 plus €16 stamp duty, €42.40 in all. Keep the receipt with its IUV code. The driving test later needs tariff N019, €16.

Step 5: File your application

Complete and sign the TT2112 form and submit it to your provincial Motorizzazione office. Add a copy of a valid ID, front and back, your codice fiscale or health card, front and back, the medical certificate, the pagoPA receipt and a 33 × 40 mm photo (some offices ask for two). A non-EU citizen also adds a copy of the residence permit. How you submit depends on the office, since some take it at the counter and others by email as a single PDF, so check yours first. The office then tells you the date of your theory exam. From filing you have 6 months and 2 attempts to pass it, and at least a month must pass between a failed attempt and the next.

It has 30 true/false questions in 20 minutes, and you may get no more than 3 wrong. It is offered in Italian, with French and German versions, and never in English. Step 7: Get your foglio rosa and take the mandatory lessons

Passing the theory exam earns the foglio rosa, a learner permit valid 12 months. Even as a private candidate you must complete 6 hours of certified lessons at an authorized driving school, in the three blocks listed for the school route, before the driving test can be booked. After the school certifies them, you may practice with a companion who sits beside you, is no older than 65, and has held a B license for at least 10 years. The car must display a "P" plate.

Step 8: Pass the driving test

The test car must have dual controls, so in practice you take it in a driving school's car, and some offices require a private candidate to go through a school for this step. You may take it from 1 month after the foglio rosa, with up to 3 attempts within its 12 months and at least a month between a failed attempt and the next. Bring the same TT2112 file, now with your foglio rosa, your lesson record and the N019 receipt. The test itself is the same as on the school route.

Step 9: Receive your license

A B license covers cars and vans up to 3.5 t with up to 9 seats, a light trailer up to 750 kg, and, in Italy only, motorcycles up to 125 cc and 11 kW. It is valid 10 years until age 50, then 5 years to 70, 3 years to 80, and 2 years after that. Cost

This is the cheapest route and the most work. The certificato anamnestico costs about €50, and the authorized doctor charges a fee of their own, plus a €16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) on the certificate. The Ministry fees are €42.40 to enroll and €16 for the driving test. The 6 mandatory hours of lessons come to roughly €300–400, and the school charges for the use of its car at the test.

How long does it take?

Usually 3–6 months start to finish. It can be as little as ~2 months if you study fast and exam slots are open, or up to ~18 months if you use the full legal windows (6 months for theory, a 12-month foglio rosa).

Already have a non-EU license (e.g. US)?

It's valid in Italy only for your first 12 months of residency (carry an International Driving Permit or a sworn Italian translation). There's no conversion agreement with the US and no exemption for experience. You still take the full theory exam, practical exam, and mandatory lessons. Once the 12 months have passed, your foreign license no longer lets you drive in Italy at all. If you are stopped driving on it, you pay a fine and the police take the license on the spot and send it to the Motorizzazione (art. 135 of the Code). If that license has also expired, you are treated as driving without a license, a far heavier matter that includes the vehicle being impounded for three months. From then on you may drive only with a foglio rosa, only after the school has certified your mandatory lessons, and only with a qualified companion beside you. That is why many people start the process early in their first year.

Wherever you can, look for an English-speaking autoscuola or instructor, and start early in your 12-month window, while your foreign license still lets you drive.

Procedures vary by region and by provincial office, and they change over time. What is described here is the common case, not a rule for every office.
