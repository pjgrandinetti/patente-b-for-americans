Fitness to Drive in Italy

Alcohol and drug limits, the effect of fatigue, medicines and illness, the blood alcohol table, and the penalties for driving unfit.

What this topic covers

This topic has two halves. The first is being fit to drive, covering fatigue, alcohol, drugs, medicines, emotional state, and eyesight. The second is first aid at a crash. Both are heavily tested and largely common-sense, but the details matter.

For US drivers. Italy's alcohol limit is far below the 0.08% BAC you know: 0.5 g/l is about 0.05%, roughly a single drink, and it is zero for your first 3 years and for professional drivers. Do not import the American calibration of "one or two drinks is fine."

Fatigue

  • If you feel tired, stop at the nearest rest area and rest. Until you get there, slow down and keep strictly to the right. Do not push on, and never close up on the vehicle ahead.
  • Tiredness, like stress, reduces attention, dulls the sense of speed and the judgment of distances, and lengthens reaction time just as alcohol does. Stress keeps nobody alert.
  • Before a long trip, set off rested and plan frequent breaks.

Alcohol

Alcohol makes driving less safe, and it improves nothing: not eyesight, not attention, not reflexes.

  • It slows reflexes and lengthens reaction times.
  • It causes drowsiness, and it does not cancel tiredness.
  • It produces euphoria and excitement that lead to imprudence and to underrating danger.
  • It impairs concentration and attention, and it worsens eyesight.
  • The legal limit is a blood-alcohol concentration of 0.5 g/l. For drivers under 21, drivers in their first 3 years with a category B license, and professional drivers, the limit is zero (0.0 g/l), so any amount is an offense.
  • After drinking too much, fitness returns only after several hours, and longer if medicines were also taken.
  • Coffee, salty food, and fresh air do not sober you up. Nothing speeds the process.
  • Drinking on an empty stomach, or together with medicines, amplifies the effect, and a large meal does not cancel a strong drink.
  • Even at a lawful level (0.5 g/l or below), or in mild intoxication, reactions are already slower and focus and coordination worse.
  • Driving while intoxicated can lead to arrest.
  • The alcohol limit applies even to non-motorized vehicles such as bicycles, and a cyclist over the limit is sanctioned too.
  • The sanctions climb in bands. Up to 0.8 g/l there is a fine, points and a license suspension of months. Above 0.8 it becomes a criminal offense with arrest. Above 1.5, or on refusing the test, the vehicle is stopped and the license can be revoked. Every band is harsher at night or after a crash, and a fatal crash can mean the crime of vehicular homicide.

Drugs and medicines

Both illegal drugs and some legal medicines impair driving.

  • Hashish, marijuana, heroin, cocaine, LSD, ecstasy, and amphetamines all compromise driving ability.
  • So can sedatives and antidepressants, and also tranquilizers, antihistamines, anti-inflammatories, painkillers and motion-sickness pills, whose side effect is drowsiness and slower reactions.
  • Coffee, carbonated drinks, mineral salts and food supplements are not among the substances that impair driving.
  • Read the leaflet, especially before a long trip, and never combine medicines with alcohol.
  • A former drug user may keep or obtain a license only by proving to the medical commission that the habit has stopped for good.

Other factors: meals, emotion, eyesight

  • A heavy or hard-to-digest meal reduces attention and lengthens reaction times. Avoid driving right after one.
  • Do not drive in an altered emotional state such as anger or strong upset. Emotions shape attention, and anxiety in particular reduces the ability to notice a hazard, so a driver who is upset is also a driver who sees less.
  • You must wear glasses or contact lenses whenever the license requires it, shown by harmonized code "01" or written out in full.
  • The glasses rule is not limited to night, to autostrade, to poor visibility, or to a certain number of diopters. If the license says so, you wear them whenever you drive.

First aid: the duty and the goal

  • Assisting an injured person is a legal obligation, not merely a moral one. It applies to everyone, not just doctors, and you may not skip it to avoid obstructing traffic or on the excuse of your own safety. You still act within your ability.
  • The duty does not depend on how serious the crash looks, and leaving the scene or waiting doing nothing is never the answer.
  • The goal of first aid is to keep the injured person alive with simple, immediate actions and to prevent further harm, assisting them as best you can while you call 112 and wait for medical help. That is the emergency number in Italy and the rest of Europe, the equivalent of 911 in the USA, and a single call reaches police, fire, or medical services. The older ambulance number, 118, still connects.
  • First aid means avoiding wrong or harmful interventions: assess the situation to protect the injured from further danger, and act within your own knowledge, never taking initiatives that belong to a doctor.
  • Asking the person how the crash happened, or their name, is not the goal of first aid and not the first thing to do.
  • Do not move the injured person unless necessary, and never if a spinal injury is possible. Giving first aid does not wait for the police to arrive. Moving the person does wait for the police, unless danger forces it.
  • Never give an injured person alcohol, and give an unconscious person nothing to drink at all. Water for a conscious person is not forbidden.
  • Do not administer drugs or perform actions that need medical training.

First aid: which position for which injury

First aid uses four positions, and each injury has its own.

  • Standing is in piedi or alzato in Italian. No injured person is kept standing or made to walk.
  • Sitting is seduto. A person with severe bleeding may sit or lie down.
  • Half-sitting is semiseduto, with the back raised and supported. It is the position for a conscious person with a chest injury, because it eases breathing.
  • Lying down is disteso, sdraiato or a terra. A person in shock lies down with the legs raised, con le gambe sollevate.
  • An unconscious person is never sat up, not even half way. Move them only if you are sure there is no spinal injury.

First aid: common situations

  • Bleeding wound. Press or tampon it with clean or, better, sterile material to stop the flow. Covering it is what stops the bleeding. Leaving it open does not.
  • If a wound is dirty with soil, rinse it with clean water, never hot water.
  • Do not remove objects embedded in a wound: cover it around them.
  • For severe bleeding, compress firmly with clean or, if possible, sterile material, have the person sit or lie down, and call 112 immediately. Dressing the wound alone is not enough, and never make them walk.
  • Foreign object in the eye. Bandage the eye and leave it alone, and tell the person not to touch it. Do not try to remove the object, rub the lid, or force the eyes open.
  • Shock shows as pale, cold, clammy skin, cold sweat on the forehead, shivering, a rapid heartbeat, and sometimes confused speech. It does not show as fever, flushing, or dark blotches.
  • Shock can come from pain, blood loss, severe trauma, or strong emotion, and it is dangerous.
  • Lay a person in shock down with legs raised. Do not make them walk, sit up, slap them, or give them alcohol.
  • Keep a person in shock warm, covered as best you can.
  • Unconscious person. Check whether they are breathing, with a hand on the lower side of the chest or on the abdomen.
  • Clear an unconscious person's nose and mouth of obstructions if breathing is hard.
  • Move an unconscious person only if you are sure there is no spinal injury.
  • Monitor an unconscious person's condition often until the ambulance arrives.
  • Give an unconscious person nothing to drink, do not tie their hands, do not bandage the head, and do not sit them up, not even half way.
  • Chest (thoracic) trauma. Help breathing by keeping a conscious victim semi-seated, not lying with legs raised and not standing (alzato), and without deep breaths.
  • Compress a deep open chest wound with a clean cloth, and stay with the person until medical help arrives.
  • Limb fractures. Do not move the limb, immobilize it, and cover any wound, and an open fracture is covered, not handled. Then, if needed, take the usual anti-shock measures.
  • Do not try to push a protruding bone back in, ask the person to move the limb, make them walk, or give them alcohol.
  • Burns. Smother any flames at once, for example with a blanket. Do not wait for them to go out.
  • Cool a burn with cold water to ease the pain, and cover it with clean or sterile material.
  • Do not pull off clothing stuck to the skin or use alcohol on the burn.

What not to do (the recurring wrong answers)

Never give an injured person alcohol. Never move them unnecessarily or with a possible spine injury. Never remove embedded objects or stuck clothing. Never give a drink to someone unconscious. And never simply leave the scene or wait doing nothing.

Summary of key points

  1. When tired, rest. Do not drive fatigued or tailgate to stay awake.
  2. Alcohol slows reflexes, causes drowsiness and false confidence, and takes hours to wear off. Coffee and food do not help. The limit is 0.5 g/l, and 0.0 for under-21, first-3-years, and professional drivers, and it covers bicycles too.
  3. Illegal drugs and some medicines such as sedatives and antidepressants impair driving. Never mix them with alcohol.
  4. Wear glasses or contacts whenever code "01" is on the license, not just at night or on certain roads.
  5. First aid is a legal duty for everyone. Keep the person alive with simple measures, prevent further harm, call 112, and do not move them if a spine injury is possible.
  6. By injury, press bleeding without removing embedded objects, bandage an injured eye, treat shock by lying the person down with legs up and keeping them warm, immobilize fractures, and for burns smother and cool without peeling stuck clothing. And never give alcohol.
