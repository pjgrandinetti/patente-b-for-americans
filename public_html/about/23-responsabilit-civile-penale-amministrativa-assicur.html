Insurance and Liability in Italy

Compulsory RCA insurance, what civil, criminal and administrative liability each mean, and the penalties for driving uninsured.

What this topic covers

This topic covers who is responsible after a crash and who pays. It includes the three kinds of liability, which are civil, criminal, and administrative, the compulsory liability insurance (RCA, Responsabilità Civile Auto), other optional coverage, and the role of driver attention. What matters is how these liabilities differ and what RCA does and does not cover.

The three liabilities

  • Civil liability (responsabilità civile) is the duty to compensate the damage a crash caused, and the damage it covers is damage to persons and to property, not to property alone.
  • Civil liability falls on both the driver and the vehicle owner, and not on the driver alone even when the driver is the one who caused the crash. The owner escapes it only by proving the vehicle was used against his will.
  • Civil liability is independent of the other two, and it is not removed because the damage was slight or because the injured person died. Being uninsured does not make it heavier either, because insurance decides who pays the compensation and not how much is owed.
  • Whoever must compensate may agree the amount directly with the injured party, may have his own vehicle repaired, and may be assisted by a road-accident claims consultant (consulente d'infortunistica stradale) in managing the claim. He cannot ask to have his license revoked instead of paying.
  • The civil obligation is discharged by performing it, so whoever must compensate the damage is free of the obligation once the injured party has been compensated.
  • Criminal liability (responsabilità penale) is personal to the driver, so it never falls on the owner for what the driver did, not even when the driver was never identified.
  • Criminal liability means the penalties the Penal Code provides, and the person who committed the crime is the one subject to them.
  • Administrative liability (responsabilità amministrativa) is the Road Code side, meaning fines, points, and suspension, and a driver who broke a Road Code rule faces it whatever else the crash brings.

A driver responsible for a crash can face all three at once. They must compensate (civil), may be prosecuted (criminal), and can lose points or the license (administrative).

Whoever is criminally and civilly responsible for a crash may also face suspension, revision, or revocation of the license, and not one of the three follows automatically. Withdrawal on the spot can precede them. No measure downgrades him to a narrower license category.

Such a driver is not always placed under preventive arrest, which is ordered only in the cases the law provides for.

When criminal liability arises or is excluded

Criminal liability arises with serious personal injury, with vehicular homicide, and with the breach of a rule of the Penal Code.

It does not arise from every consequence of a crash. It does not arise from damage to persons or property as such, nor from the vehicle being completely destroyed, nor from every breach of the Road Code, which is an offense without being a crime in itself.

It is excluded when the damage is involuntary and to property only, when the act is not a crime, and when it results from force majeure, meaning an unforeseeable and unavoidable event beyond the driver's control such as an earthquake or a flood.

It is not excluded by anything done after the crash, so neither helping the victim, nor paying the damage, nor the insurer compensating it removes it.

Compulsory RCA insurance

RCA (Responsabilità Civile Auto, or third-party motor liability) is the insurance that pays the civil compensation the liable person owes. The rules below say what it must cover, what it never covers, and what happens once it expires.

  • RCA is mandatory by law, and it covers the damage your vehicle causes to others, both to persons and to property, so no separate contract is needed for property damage.
  • It covers the liability of both the driver and the owner, whoever of them is at the wheel.
  • It is valid throughout the European Union, so it answers for a crash in another member state.
  • It covers the passengers carried whenever the vehicle is authorized to carry persons, and not only the passengers of a taxi.
  • The insurance certificate must always be kept in the vehicle.
  • RCA does not cover criminal liability, whatever premium was paid for it.
  • It does not cover the administrative fines the Road Code imposes.
  • It does not cover damage above the policy limit, which the liable person must pay himself.
  • The policy limits (massimali) are the most the insurer will pay for a claim, and not the money the insurer collects from the client each year. Your own liability has no such limit, so whatever the damage exceeds the limit by is yours to pay.
  • The legal minimum for the limits is fixed by law, and the client may raise them by paying a higher premium.
  • The limits do not rise every year, and they are never refunded at year end to a client who caused no accidents.
  • Bonus-malus makes the premium rise or fall with the number of at-fault accidents, and the insurer sets it, not a judge.
  • Bonus-malus applies to motorcycles as well, and the contract is never cancelled once a certain number of accidents is reached.
  • Once the contract is signed, the insurer must issue the insurance certificate, and not a list of repair shops, a first-aid booklet, or a vehicle manual.
  • Direct compensation (risarcimento diretto: claiming from your own insurer) applies when there is damage to the vehicle and at most slight injury to the driver.
  • Direct compensation is available only when no more than two vehicles are involved, motorcycles included, and the claim needs no photographs of the damage.
  • When those conditions are not met, with three or more vehicles involved for example, the claim follows the ordinary route instead and goes to the insurer of the vehicle responsible for the damage. That is the same cover described above, since RCA exists to pay for the damage a vehicle causes to others.
  • Using a vehicle with no insurance cover on public roads is forbidden, and driving or parking one there brings a fine.
  • Once the RCA has expired you may neither drive nor even park the vehicle on a public road, and it can be confiscated.
  • Since December 2023 the duty to insure also covers a vehicle kept on private land, parked in a garage or used only in areas closed to the public, so keeping it off public roads does not remove the need for insurance. The exceptions are a vehicle formally withdrawn from circulation, one no longer fit for use as a means of transport, and one whose policy the owner has suspended through the insurer.
  • Nothing shortens that ban, neither a short trip, nor staying within Italy, nor a promise to pay any damage out of your own pocket.
  • Putting the vehicle back on the road takes valid insurance, and no technical inspection is required for it.

Other coverage and the guarantee fund

  • Policies for theft, fire, vandalism, natural events, or damage to your own vehicle (kasko) are optional, so none of them is required by law.
  • A theft or fire payout cannot exceed the vehicle's value at the time of the loss, and such a policy pays a sum of money rather than a life annuity to the owner.
  • A fire policy normally covers accidental fires as well as deliberate ones, so the cover is not limited to arson.
  • No anti-theft device and no battery cut-off is needed in order to take out a theft or fire policy.
  • A theft abroad must be reported again in Italy before the policy can be activated.
  • The Road Accident Victims Guarantee Fund compensates people injured by uninsured vehicles, by unidentified vehicles, and by vehicles insured with an insolvent insurer.
  • The fund is not a reward for accident-free driving, not a life annuity for an injured driver, and not a scheme you join by choice. It is paid for by a levy on every RCA premium. In Italian premio means both a reward and an insurance premium, and the fund is neither.
  • The fund does not pay for damage caused by underage drivers.

Attention and distraction

Distraction is one of the main causes of crashes. The road and the other users already claim all the attention you have, so anything else you do at the wheel takes a share of it away.

  • The attention you can give the road is limited, and it is never unlimited. It can be trained and improved with practice, which raises the limit without removing it.
  • The senses cannot register every stimulus around the vehicle, and a stimulus too faint to cross the threshold of a sense is not perceived at all.
  • Because perception has those limits, both the perception of a hazard and the reaction that follows it depend on the conditions around the vehicle, such as the light, the weather and the noise.
  • Hearing matters as much as sight at the wheel, because a horn or a siren often reaches you before the vehicle sounding it comes into view.
  • Attention has to cover the whole scene, the users ahead of you and those at the edges of the road, who may move onto the carriageway. Watching only the few meters of pavement in front of the hood is not enough, because at driving speed you cross that strip before you could react to anything in it.
  • Assess what the other road users are doing and allow for it, because you are the one who will have to deal with the consequences of their mistakes. This holds even when they are the ones breaking the rules.
  • A phone reduces attention even with a hands-free kit. Setting the navigator, tuning the radio by hand, very loud music, and arguing with passengers or carrying noisy ones all take a share of it, and a task you are used to still costs you attention.
  • Never look away from the road, even for milliseconds, and at an intersection keep maximum concentration. Stop and wait if you are not sure you can cross safely.
  • Never overestimate your driving ability. A driver judges his own driving generously, and that is how he ends up in a situation harder to handle than he expected.
  • Judge a hazard for what it is, without inflating it and without playing it down. Underrating danger to keep anxiety away leaves you unprepared for the danger itself.
  • Do not let passengers set your speed. A request to go faster is not a reason to go faster, and the person responsible for how the vehicle is driven is the driver, not the passenger.
  • The rules of circulation exist to make driving safer for every road user, so following them lowers the risk for motorized and non-motorized vehicles alike and limits the obstruction of traffic that violations cause.
  • They protect drivers as much as the vulnerable users, not one of them is a secondary rule to be set aside when it suits you, and they apply always, not only in heavy traffic or when a fine is likely. A ban does not lapse because you have waited a long time behind the vehicle it keeps you behind.

Summary of key points

  1. Civil liability means compensating the damage, falling on driver and owner. Criminal liability is personal to the driver, for crimes like injury or homicide. Administrative liability is Road Code fines, points, and suspension.
  2. Criminal liability is not erased by paying, helping the victim, or insurance. It is excluded for property-only damage, force majeure, or when the act is not a crime.
  3. RCA is compulsory, covers third-party damage to persons and property, is EU-valid, and the certificate stays in the vehicle. It does not cover fines, criminal liability, or damage over the limit.
  4. Limits have a legal minimum and can be raised with a higher premium, and bonus-malus ties the premium to your accident record.
  5. With expired RCA you may not drive or park on public roads, with a confiscation risk. Kasko, theft, and fire policies are optional, and the guarantee fund pays victims of uninsured or unidentified vehicles.
  6. Distraction is a leading crash cause, and the attention you can give the road is limited. Phones even with hands-free, the navigator, tuning the radio by hand, loud music, and arguments all impair attention. Never look away, never overrate your ability, and watch the other users as well as the road ahead.
