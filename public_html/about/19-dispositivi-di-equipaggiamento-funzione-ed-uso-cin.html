Seat Belts and Child Seats in Italy

Who must wear a belt and when, how children are restrained by height and age, airbags, helmets, and the reflective vest and warning triangle.

What this topic covers

This topic covers the safety gear in and on a vehicle. It includes seatbelts, child restraints, the airbag, the headrest, the helmet, and a motorcyclist's protective clothing. What matters is when each is mandatory and that you may not disable or improvise it.

For US drivers. There is no state-by-state variation here: the helmet is mandatory for every rider and passenger on every moped and motorcycle, everywhere, and seatbelts are mandatory in all seats, front and rear. Child restraints go by height (up to 1.50 m), not by age alone.

Seatbelts

What a seatbelt does

  • A seatbelt exists to limit injury to the driver and passengers in a crash.
  • In a violent impact it keeps the body against the seat.
  • It stops the occupants from striking the inside of the vehicle.
  • It stops them from being thrown out of the car.
  • Without it, even a sudden brake can throw you against the windshield.

Who wears it, and when

  • The belt must be worn by the driver and by every passenger of a car fitted with belts, in the rear seats as much as the front. Wearing it in the back is an obligation, not a recommendation.
  • It must be worn in every driving situation.
  • There is no speed below which the belt may stay off, so a rule of "only at 30 km/h or more" does not exist.
  • There is no trip short enough to go without it, not even a few hundred meters inside town, and it is far from useless in city traffic.
  • An airbag, front or side, does not remove the obligation.
  • The driver must see that every passenger is belted, but answers for the minors only. An adult passenger answers for himself.

Keeping it working

  • Belts must be of an approved type and kept efficient at all times.
  • They need a periodic check for wear and efficiency.
  • Altering or obstructing a belt's normal working is penalized, even by someone who is wearing it.
  • The warning light for an unfastened belt may not be disabled from the dashboard.
  • The retractor may not be disabled or modified, however much it bothers a passenger.
  • No device may hold the belt away from the body, not even for an obese person.

The exemptions

  • A woman with a certified at-risk pregnancy is exempt.
  • A person with a certified illness that rules the belt out is exempt.
  • Driving instructors are exempt while teaching. The Code adds refuse collectors on their rounds and the crews of emergency vehicles on duty.
  • A mail carrier on the job is not exempt.
  • Age is not an exemption, whether over 65 or over 70.
  • A learner driving on the foglio rosa is not exempt.

Child restraints

  • A child under 1.50 m (1.6 yd) must be secured by an approved child restraint suited to their height and weight.
  • That holds on urban trips, at any speed, and in the front seats as in the back, even within the driver's reach.
  • A child under 1.50 m may never ride in an adult's arms, in front or in the back. A belted adult does not make it safe.
  • The adult belt is no substitute for the restraint, whatever the child's height below 1.50 m.
  • A child under 3 may not travel at all in a car that carries no suitable restraint. A child over 3 may, but only in a rear seat until they pass 1.50 m.
  • A child restraint must be of an approved type, which its label shows with a capital E, and must be kept in working order.
  • It may never be home-made, and no health-authority expert can approve one.
  • Restraints are not one single type: the seat is chosen by the child's size and changed as the child grows.
  • The current i-Size standard sizes a seat by the child's height, under 100 cm and from 100 to 150 cm, and a seat chosen that way must face rearward until 15 months.
  • In a taxi or a hired car with driver, a child under 1.50 m may ride without a restraint, but only in a rear seat and accompanied by a passenger aged at least 16.
  • In a car with a passenger airbag, a child seat goes on the front seat only if the car has a control to switch that airbag off, and a rear-facing seat only after it has been switched off.
  • If the car has no way to switch off the passenger airbag, a rear-facing seat goes in the back.
  • A child under 4 in a child seat must be protected by an anti-abandonment alarm device that warns the driver not to leave the child behind in the vehicle.

The airbag

  • The airbag is an inflatable cushion of nylon or another synthetic material.
  • It fires in a road crash with a violent impact.
  • Its job is to limit injuries from striking the inside surfaces of the vehicle in such an impact.
  • A sensor triggers it automatically. There is no button on the dashboard.
  • It inflates in a few milliseconds, not seconds.
  • It supplements the seatbelt and is used together with it.
  • It can be dangerous if it fires while you are not belted.
  • It is useful on every road, in town as much as outside it.
  • It can be fitted on any car, whatever the number of doors.
  • It can sometimes be deactivated, which is what a rear-facing child seat up front needs.
  • The driver cannot set the impact speed at which it fires.
  • It is never inflated to test it.

The headrest

  • The headrest is a safety device, not a styling one.
  • It helps prevent whiplash when the car is hit from behind.
  • It works only when adjusted correctly in height and angle, with its top level with the top of your head and its face no more than 10 cm behind it.
  • It is not there to relax the muscles while driving.

The helmet

Who must wear it

  • The helmet is mandatory for the rider of a two-wheeled moped.
  • It is mandatory for the rider of a motorcycle without a safety cell and restraint devices.
  • It is mandatory for the passenger too, on a motorcycle without a safety cell as on a two-wheeled moped, and being an adult changes nothing.
  • It is required for everyone, not only minors.
  • Holding a category B license does not exempt the rider of a moped.
  • Years of experience do not exempt an adult either.
  • It must be worn on short trips and at low speed too.
  • A 30 km/h zone is no exception.
  • Hot weather is no exception.
  • What decides it on a three- or four-wheeler is whether the vehicle protects you itself. A quadricycle with no closed body, no safety cell and no restraint devices, the quad, requires the helmet; one whose closed body and seatbelts already hold you requires none. So the answer for quadricycles is "it depends on the body", never "always".
  • Riding without a helmet costs license points.

Wearing it

  • Its purpose is to reduce the effect of blows to the head.
  • It must be type-approved (omologato: certified against the official safety standard and marked accordingly; European English calls this "homologated"). Mopeds get no exception.
  • It must be the correct size.
  • It must be correctly fastened whenever you ride, in town as well as outside it.
  • Worn wrongly or left unfastened, it can cost you serious injury in a fall.

Care and replacement

  • Replace it after a strong impact, even if the outer shell shows no deformation.
  • It ages with light and heat, so even without an impact it is replaced after about five years, not kept for decades.
  • Clean it with non-corrosive detergent.
  • Never clean it with solvents.
  • Never clean it with gasoline or paint thinner, not even to remove insects.
  • The solvents in paints and glues can weaken the outer shell.
  • No stickers go on the helmet, and it is not painted by hand.
  • A cracked shell is not repaired with tape, however small the crack.

Full-face helmets and the visor

  • A full-face helmet also protects the face from injury in a crash.
  • It may be worn in dense city traffic like anywhere else.
  • It is just as useful on a moped.
  • What the law demands is the approved helmet itself, correctly fastened, and it says nothing about a visor, so a visor is advisable rather than required.
  • The visor shields the face and eyes from insects.
  • It must give perfect vision in any light, so it does not worsen visibility and is worn as much as possible.
  • Replace it when scratched or damaged, never patch it with tape.
  • The helmet's ventilation limits fogging of the visor.

E-scooters (monopattini)

The 2024 reform added requirements for electric e-scooters.

  • Every rider must now wear a helmet at any age, where previously only minors had to.
  • The e-scooter itself must carry an identification marker (plate) and be covered by insurance.

Other protective gear

Approval

  • Approved motorcycle clothing is tested for cut resistance and impact-abrasion resistance.
  • It is not tested for washing.
  • It comes with padding for the season, in summer and winter versions.
  • Color is not a requirement, so black is not what makes a jacket approved.

Jackets

  • A motorcycle jacket protects against abrasion when sliding on the asphalt.
  • It has rigid inserts at the joints.

Gloves

  • Gloves are advisable on mopeds and motorcycles.
  • They are advisable in summer too, since their main purpose is protection, not warmth.
  • Approved gloves protect the back of the hand from hard blows.
  • They protect the palm when sliding on the asphalt.
  • They keep control feel and give a surer grip, so bare hands are not quicker on the controls.
  • They have fastening systems that stop them slipping off in a fall.
  • Their padding varies with the season.
  • Approved motorcycle gloves exist.
  • Ski gloves are no substitute, not even in winter.
  • They are not recognized by a color or a printed logo.
  • They cover the hand, not the elbow.
  • They are suitable for a moped as for a motorcycle.

Boots

  • Motorcycle boots protect the feet and ankles of a rider.
  • They reduce sprains from a misplaced foot and protect the ankle joint from the cold.
  • They protect the feet from abrasion on the asphalt in a fall.
  • They have no loose straps or laces that could catch on the pedals, so double laces are not among their features.
  • They are approved under international standards.
  • Approved boots are designed not to come off in a fall.
  • They cover the leg below the knee, not above it.
  • The sole is not steel.
  • They are not made of fireproof rubber.
  • Specific motorcycle boots do exist, and hiking, hunting or climbing shoes are no substitute.

Summary of key points

  1. Wear seatbelts always, in all seats, with no exceptions for short trips, low speed, or having an airbag. Keep them approved and efficient, and do not disable them. Exemptions are only certified medical or pregnancy cases, driving instructors, refuse collectors on their rounds and emergency crews on duty.
  2. Children under 1.50 m need an approved restraint, and under 3 cannot travel without one. A child under 4 in a seat needs an anti-abandonment alarm. A rear-facing seat goes up front only with the airbag off, and never carry a child in someone's arms.
  3. The airbag supplements the belt, fires in milliseconds, can be dangerous without the belt, and can sometimes be deactivated.
  4. The headrest prevents whiplash when adjusted for height and angle.
  5. The helmet is mandatory for moped and motorcycle riders and passengers, and open quads, at any age, speed, or trip length. Replace it after an impact, clean it without solvents, and keep it type-approved and fastened.
  6. Approved boots, gloves, and jackets give real protection and cannot be improvised. Wear gloves even in summer.
