Passing and Overtaking Rules in Italy

When you may pass, which side you pass on, where passing is forbidden outright, and what the vehicle being passed has to do.

What this topic is about

Passing is moving past a vehicle going the same direction. The Italian word is sorpasso (verb sorpassare), and it means only this maneuver. Do not confuse it with superare, the general verb "to get past": superare la striscia is crossing the line, superare il limite is exceeding the limit; the bank does sometimes say superare un veicolo for overtaking, but sorpassare never means anything else. Passing is normally done on the left, and it is one of the most dangerous maneuvers, because the risk comes from vehicles both ahead and behind that may intend to do the same thing. Most questions test three things: the checks before you start, where it is forbidden, and the exceptions that let you pass on the right.

For US drivers. Two habits to unlearn. In the US, passing on the right is legal in two cases: when the vehicle ahead is turning left, and on roads with two or more lanes in your direction, where most states permit a right-lane pass done safely (never on the shoulder). Italy keeps only the first case: even on a multilane road, passing on the right is prohibited, with the narrow exceptions of a vehicle ahead signaling a left turn and channelized or parallel-lines traffic. Lane discipline follows directly: the "keep right except to pass" rule that many American drivers ignore is strictly the law in Italy: you must return to the rightmost free lane after overtaking. In the US a driver who lingers in the left lane is merely rude, because others can legally pass on the right; in Italy nobody may pass you on the right, so lingering in the left lane truly blocks the road, and it is fined. And overtaking here often means crossing into the oncoming lane, so the visibility and return rules are stricter than anything the US tests, and heavily examined. A third habit to unlearn: the US rule is about the opposing lane. Typical state law forbids driving to the left of center within 100 feet of an intersection or through one, and lifts that limit on a one-way street. Italy forbids the maneuver itself at and near a junction, even inside your own half, then allows it in four cases (below). An officer waving you past will feel familiar; a traffic light doing the same has no US counterpart.

Before you start: the checks

Passing is never just "nothing is coming the other way." Before pulling out you must be sure of several things.

  • Visibility and a clear road ahead let you complete the pass, oncoming traffic included.
  • The carriageway is wide enough for the maneuver in safety.
  • The maneuver can be completed without obstructing anyone.
  • No sign forbids passing here.
  • The vehicle ahead has not signaled the same maneuver, and the vehicle behind has not already begun to pass you.
  • You have judged the space needed from the speed difference and the lengths of both vehicles.
  • You keep your safety distance even as you begin.
  • You must signal left with the turn signal first.
  • You may flash the high beams to warn the vehicle ahead, allowed day or night.
  • The horn is not required, and signaling alone is never enough on its own. Nor does your engine's power come into it.

How to pass and return

  • Pass on the left, and keep an adequate lateral gap from the vehicle you pass.
  • Adjust your speed to that of the vehicle you are passing, so as to clear it promptly: you do accelerate, within the limits, and no rule sets a speed twice the other's. Keeping strictly right is for the vehicle being passed, not for you.
  • When the vehicle you are passing is a bicycle, the gap must be wide. Since the 2024 reform the Code asks for a lateral clearance of at least 1.5 meters (1.6 yd) wherever road conditions allow, because cyclists are less stable; the Ministry's bank predates that figure and marks only a quoted "at least 2 m" false.
  • Afterward, return to the right as soon as you safely can, but never cut in so sharply that the vehicle you passed must brake, and never by slowing down. No fixed distance, such as 100 m, governs the return, and no horn announces it.
  • Check your mirrors before moving back.
  • On a road with at least two lanes in your direction, you may make a second pass without first returning right, provided you obstruct no one.

When you pass on the right

The left rule has real exceptions. Passing on the right is sometimes correct, not forbidden.

  • You pass on the right when the vehicle ahead has signaled a left turn and begun it.
  • On a one-way road, you pass on the right when the vehicle ahead has signaled pulling over to the left.
  • A moving tram may be passed on the right where the width of the carriageway to its right allows, since it runs on fixed rails down the road; on a one-way road either side is allowed.
  • A tram or trolleybus stopped at a stop for passengers may be passed on the right only if there is a safety island (salvagente), and not if there is none.
  • A trolleybus runs on tires, so in motion it is an ordinary vehicle, passed on the left.

Where passing is forbidden

  • Passing is forbidden near curves and crests on a two-lane, two-way road. The ban covers the approach and top of the crest, and it holds for every vehicle you might pass, an animal-drawn cart or a stopped bus included.
  • It is forbidden in poor visibility, and flashing the lights during the pass changes nothing.
  • On the descending stretch after a crest, where the road ahead is visible again, passing is allowed even on a two-lane two-way road.
  • It is forbidden at and near an intersection (in corrispondenza o in prossimità di un'intersezione), because a junction can release traffic across your path from a direction you cannot see. This ban is conditional: four things lift it, and they are listed in the next section.
  • It is forbidden where it would require crossing a continuous center line (striscia continua).
  • It is forbidden on acceleration or deceleration lanes (corsia di accelerazione o decelerazione).
  • It is forbidden at or near a railroad crossing, as at an intersection. At an unguarded crossing, one without barriers and without a traffic light, the ban holds even if you stay within your own half.
  • You may not pass a vehicle that is itself already passing if you would need the oncoming lane.
  • You may not pass stationary or slow vehicles at a railroad crossing (passaggio a livello), at traffic lights, or in congestion when it would require moving into the oncoming lane. The test is the half of the road reserved for oncoming traffic, not a painted line: in a queue at a light or through a junction there is often no line to read, and the ban applies just the same. Stay in your own half and this particular rule does not forbid the pass.
  • You may not pass a vehicle that has stopped or slowed to let pedestrians cross. This is forbidden even away from a marked crossing.
  • You may not pass by tailgating an ambulance on an emergency call, or behind any emergency vehicle with sirens on, to slip through traffic.
  • At night on a two-lane two-way extra-urban road a pass is not forbidden as such, but the reduced visibility calls for particular care, and you give it up if the vehicle ahead accelerates.
  • Passing in the wake (in scia) means closely following the vehicle ahead through its own overtake, to pass with it. This is an overtake of your own, so wherever a pass is forbidden, following another vehicle through it is forbidden too.

Where it is allowed despite a hazard

  • Passing is allowed in a curve or over a crest, but only if the road has at least two lanes in your direction, with separate carriageways or without, because the oncoming danger is removed. Two lanes shared by both directions are not enough.
  • It is allowed at or near an intersection in four cases.
  1. Traffic is governed by a traffic light or a traffic officer.
  2. You are on a priority road (strada a precedenza) that also has two separate carriageways, is one-way, or has at least two marked lanes your way. Priority alone is not enough.
  3. The vehicle ahead has signaled a left turn and begun it, and you pass it on the right.
  4. You are passing a bicycle (veicolo a due ruote non a motore) without moving into the oncoming half.

That last condition belongs to the bicycle alone. A separate rule limits the others: traffic stopped or crawling at a light, a railroad crossing or in a queue may not be passed by crossing into the oncoming half, so a light does not let you drive around the line of stopped cars. So a light does not forbid passing at a junction, and passing is not made lawful merely because nobody is coming from the right.

  • It is allowed at a railroad crossing without barriers when traffic is regulated by traffic lights.
  • On a one-way road, curves and crests do not forbid passing, because there is no oncoming traffic.

The driver being passed

  • The vehicle being passed must make it easier, not harder: do not accelerate. Racing the passing driver is forbidden.
  • On a road with one lane each way, the vehicle being passed keeps as far right as possible: never to the center, and no hazard lights or lower gear are asked of it.
  • A very slow vehicle must go further and stop as soon as possible to let the queue flow.
  • If you are being passed just as an oncoming vehicle appears, slow down and hug the right edge. Do not flash, signal right, or hold your speed.
  • If a crash follows and you did nothing to help the maneuver, you can share the liability.

Space, and some common misconceptions

  • The space you need grows when the vehicle ahead accelerates, when either vehicle is longer, and when the speed difference is small, because you spend longer alongside. It shrinks if the vehicle ahead slows.
  • The speed limits still apply while you pass. Overtaking never licenses exceeding them.

Watch out for these false beliefs.

  • It is not always permitted to pass a two-wheeler.
  • Passing a bus stopped at a stop is not forbidden as such; the restriction is on passing a stopped tram or trolleybus on the right, which requires a safety island.
  • Passing police vehicles is not forbidden, unless they are on an emergency response.
  • Passing is not forbidden on one-way roads, in snow, near hospitals, service stations, driveways or parking-lot exits as such, nor in town at night because the lighting is poor.
  • A vehicle that has signaled a right turn, or that is pulling to the right edge, may still be passed on the left.
  • On the autostrada you may pass only on the left, not on either side, and do not linger in the passing lane.
  • Motorcycles are no exception: they pass on the left like everyone else, not on both sides, not on the right where there is room, and never along a reserved lane to beat the traffic.
  • A dashed center line may be crossed to pass. Only a continuous one may not. Where a dashed line runs alongside a solid one, what counts is the line nearer to you: if that one is dashed, you may cross.

What makes overtaking dangerous

One question family asks what the danger of overtaking consists of. The true factors are moving conflicts: a head-on collision with oncoming traffic, hitting the overtaken vehicle on approach or on the return to the lane, other vehicles signaling the same intention, and a narrow carriageway. Fixed roadside equipment, such as ordinary edge markers, is not a danger factor. Each statement names one factor; judge each on its own, since several are true.

On the autostrada the same conflicts take other forms, such as too little attention to the vehicles behind, too short a following distance, a vehicle ahead swinging suddenly into the passing lane, lingering in the passing lane, following another vehicle through its overtake, and a strong side wind that unsettles the vehicles. A rest area nearby, or low beams on by day, are not among them.

A grammar note for reading the Italian. These statements read la pericolosità del sorpasso è determinata da.... Italian requires the definite article before abstract nouns, so la pericolosità carries no suggestion of a single cause, and è determinata da means "comes from," not "is solely determined by." Do not let the English instinct that "the danger" names one exclusive cause push you toward false; the intended trap in this family is the false factor, not the article.

Summary of key points

  1. Pass on the left by default. First confirm visibility, width, a clear road to finish, no prohibiting sign, and that no one ahead or behind is already maneuvering.
  2. Signal left. Flashing headlights is allowed, and the horn is not required.
  3. Pass on the right when the vehicle ahead is turning or pulling left, and for trams (on the right, and only with a safety island when the tram is stopped).
  4. Passing is forbidden near curves and crests and in poor visibility on two-lane two-way roads, at and near intersections, across a continuous line, on acceleration or deceleration lanes, and when passing stopped or slow vehicles at crossings, lights, or congestion, or for pedestrians, if it needs the oncoming lane.
  5. Passing is allowed in curves and crests on separated multi-lane roads. At a junction it is allowed under lights or an officer, on a priority road that also has separate carriageways or two marked lanes your way, behind a vehicle turning left, or for a bicycle if you stay in your own half. Whatever the case, vehicles stopped or crawling at a light or in a queue may never be passed by crossing into the oncoming half.
  6. The vehicle being passed must not accelerate and should keep right; a very slow one stops when it can. When you are being passed as oncoming traffic appears, slow down and keep right, or you risk shared liability.
  7. The descending side of a crest is passable even on a two-lane two-way road; speed limits apply throughout the pass.
  8. When you pass a bicycle, leave at least 1.5 meters (1.6 yd) of lateral clearance where the road allows.
