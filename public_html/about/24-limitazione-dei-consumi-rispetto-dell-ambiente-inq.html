Fuel Economy and Pollution in Italy

What this topic covers

This topic covers how to use less fuel and how to limit the three kinds of pollution a vehicle causes, which are air, noise, and waste. The rules are mostly about smooth driving and good maintenance.

This air pollution is due mostly to the exhaust gases that combustion generates inside the engine. What a vehicle emits therefore depends on how that combustion goes.

The air pollution produced by vehicles harms people directly, because it damages the respiratory tract and irritates the eyes.

The same exhaust gases act on a larger scale as well, and they increase smog, acid rain, and the greenhouse effect.

Saving fuel

  • Keep a steady, moderate speed and drive smoothly, avoiding hard acceleration and braking.
  • Consumption rises above all with speed, and also with weight and with a trailer in tow.
  • Consumption also rises on hilly or congested roads, so prefer flat, free-flowing routes when you can.
  • Shift up as soon as a low gear is no longer needed, and keep the engine at moderate revs. The higher the revs, the more fuel the engine burns and the more it emits.
  • Change gear with a light foot, easing off the accelerator instead of pressing it. During the shift the clutch disconnects the engine from the wheels, so extra throttle burns fuel and makes noise without moving the vehicle.
  • Reduce weight and drag. Do not carry a roof rack or luggage on the roof, clear snow off the roof, and keep the windows and sunroof closed at medium-high speed.
  • Keep the tire pressure correct, since under-inflation raises consumption. Use the air conditioning sparingly, and remember that electrical loads such as lights and the heated rear window add to consumption.
  • Fuel that is burned leaves the engine as exhaust gas, so anything that raises consumption raises emissions too. Correct tire pressure and sparing use of the air conditioner, which the engine has to drive, therefore cut air pollution as well.
  • Do not rev a cold or stationary engine. Pressing the accelerator with the engine cold or stopped, in neutral or with the clutch down, only raises the revs: it wastes fuel and adds pollution and noise, and it neither saves fuel nor warms the engine faster.

Reducing air pollution

  • Drive smoothly, do not rev a cold engine, turn the engine off during long stops, and avoid using the car for very short trips.
  • Maintain the engine. Air pollution depends on how well the engine is running, so keeping the vehicle in optimal consumption and performance condition reduces it.
  • Keep the air filter clean and have it replaced periodically. A dirty or clogged filter starves the engine of air and upsets combustion, which raises emissions.
  • Efficient injectors keep emissions down, because they meter the fuel so that it burns completely.
  • On a diesel, have the injection pump and injectors checked periodically.
  • Have the catalytic converter replaced when it is deteriorated or spent, since it is the device that treats the exhaust gases.
  • The catalytic converter works properly only at high temperature, so pollution is greater right after a cold start.
  • Dark exhaust smoke means more pollution, because it is the visible residue of incomplete combustion. Have the engine checked whenever the smoke turns too dark.
  • Air pollution comes from combustion and from the exhaust, so the parts to service are the air filter, the fuel system, and the catalytic converter. The clutch, differential, and shock absorbers transmit motion and control the ride, so they leave the exhaust gases untouched.
  • The oil filter is replaced at the scheduled maintenance intervals set for the vehicle.
  • Choosing a vehicle powered by electricity, methane (CNG), or LPG reduces air pollution, because these are cleaner power sources than gasoline or diesel. An electric vehicle emits no fumes at all.
  • A hybrid carries a combustion engine plus an electric motor and pollutes less, because the electric motor takes over part of the work and recovers the energy of braking.
  • Less vehicle weight lowers air pollution, because a lighter vehicle asks less work of the engine and burns less fuel.
  • Euro standards work so that a higher Euro number is cleaner. "Euro 0" is not zero-emission, and newer standards pollute less.
  • Driving a vehicle whose exhaust exceeds the limits is an offense. It carries an administrative fine and the withdrawal of the registration document (ritiro della carta di circolazione), which keeps the vehicle off the road until it is put right.
  • You may not modify the engine or the exhaust to change what the vehicle emits. Driving a vehicle whose engine or exhaust has been modified carries heavy administrative penalties.
  • The pollen (cabin) filter cleans the air inside the cabin, so replacing it protects the people on board rather than the outside air.
  • During a long stop in a tunnel, switching on air recirculation is good for your health, because it keeps exhaust fumes out of the cabin.

Reducing noise pollution

  • Keep the muffler efficient and of an approved type. Never drill it, cut it, or fit a smaller pipe.
  • Use the horn only for safety, such as an immediate danger or the transport of an injured or seriously ill person. Sound it as little as possible, because every extra blast is avoidable noise.
  • Audible warning devices are allowed on the roads in general: in built-up areas the horn is for real and immediate danger only, and everywhere it is sounded as briefly as possible.
  • Flashing the headlights is the quieter warning, and it serves at any hour, not only at night.
  • Correct driving keeps vehicle noise down. Accelerate gently and brake gradually, braking hard only when safety strictly requires it.
  • With the vehicle stationary the engine turns without moving it, so leave the accelerator alone. During a stop in traffic, let the engine idle quietly rather than revving it.
  • Close the doors gently. They latch without force, so closing them softly removes an avoidable noise.
  • Have noisy brakes checked, and replaced if necessary. Noise during braking often means wear, so the check removes a noise source and protects braking safety at the same time.
  • Worn suspension adds noise as well, so keeping it in good condition helps keep the vehicle quiet.
  • Noise also comes from tire scrub in fast curves and empty trailers bouncing, not just the engine.

Waste and the environment

  • Do not dump antifreeze, engine oil, or a battery on the ground or into drains, because they harm soil and groundwater. Hand a used battery back to the dealer and take engine oil to proper disposal, avoiding skin contact. The retailer who sells you a new battery takes the exhausted one back at no extra cost.
  • Scrap a vehicle only at an authorized collection or dismantling center (not necessarily the manufacturer's dealer), and empty motorhome waste at proper sanitary points.
  • Do not litter the road, throw objects from a moving vehicle, or drive on with mud-caked wheels, and do not damage road works and installations. Abandoning hazardous waste (oil, fluids, repair leftovers) on the ground is punished.

Summary of key points

  1. Pressing the accelerator on a cold or stationary engine, or in neutral or with the clutch down, only revs it. It wastes fuel and adds pollution and noise, and it never saves fuel or warms the engine faster.
  2. Save fuel with a steady moderate speed, smooth driving, less weight and roof load, correct tire pressure, closed windows at speed, and sparing AC use.
  3. Cut air pollution with smooth driving, a well-maintained engine (filter, injectors, catalytic converter), cleaner fuels, and the engine off during long stops. A higher Euro number is cleaner, and over-polluting brings fines.
  4. Cut noise with an efficient approved muffler, never drilled or cut, minimal horn use, no revving when stopped, and good brakes.
  5. Dispose properly. Do not dump oil, antifreeze, or batteries, return used batteries to the dealer, scrap at an authorized center, and empty motorhome waste at sanitary points.
  6. Do not litter or throw objects from the vehicle.
