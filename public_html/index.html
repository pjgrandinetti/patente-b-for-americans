The Patente B study app written for Americans. Every official question in English and Italian, with the rule behind each answer, online or offline.

How to use this app

Who this is for. This app was built for American English speakers moving to Italy. The US has no license-swap agreement with Italy, so you must pass the Italian theory exam like a first-time driver, and the exam is given only in Italian. (Drivers from Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa are in the same position. UK, Irish, and Maltese licenses convert directly.) The app therefore teaches two things at once, the road rules and the Italian you need to read the questions, and it explains Italian rules through their closest US equivalents.

What you are working through. The full official bank is 7,106 true/false questions on 849 cards. These are the questions the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport publishes for the category B exam, taken from its list of 23 April 2025, and their Italian wording and answers are reproduced unchanged. The Ministry publishes the lists on its Portale dell’Automobilista. By default the app runs in Abridged mode, which keeps every distinct idea and drops the near-duplicate wordings, about 4,350 questions. See Settings (⚙️) for details.

Quick start. Press ▶ Begin in the top bar. It opens the first card you have not yet answered, at the English level, and from then on it reads Resume and takes you back to where you left off. The rest of this page explains what you will see.

1. The three levels. Switch between them at any time with the English / Italian / Italiano buttons. Each keeps its own progress, and a question you get right at a higher level also counts as cleared at the levels below it.

  • English. Every question is shown in Italian with its English translation.
  • Italian. Questions are in Italian only, but tapping an underlined word shows its English meaning. Each word you ask about joins your vocabulary study list.
  • Italiano. Questions are in Italian only, with no help, as in the real exam.

Work a topic in English first, then redo it in Italian, then in Italiano. If you are already fluent, start at Italiano, since clearing a card there clears it everywhere.

2. How long it takes. The whole job is every topic cleared at every level you use, so an English speaker answers each question correctly three times. This is far more than a US written test asks, and Italian candidates spend months on it in their own language. At five days a week, the months to reach the exam are:

3. Working a topic. While ▶ Begin (or Resume) takes you directly to the first unanswered question, when you are starting a new topic we recommend reading its Concepts first, from the topic page. While you work, every question carries a 📖 that opens the Concepts at the paragraph answering that question, and tapping a question's picture opens them at the section that explains that sign. A card is cleared once you answer all its questions correctly, so you keep seeing only the cards you miss until the whole topic is cleared. At English and Italian the Concepts are written in English, and an IT button on each line shows that line in Italian. At Italiano it is the other way round: the Concepts open in Italian, with an EN button on each line, so the notes become reading practice too.

4. The Reference page. 📗 Reference is a handy page you can refer to when answering questions, to quickly look up the various numerical quantities needed for the exam. Unfortunately, such a "cheat sheet" is not allowed during the actual exam, so ultimately you will need to memorize as many of these quantities as you can before taking it. This page also lists the official questions whose wording is 🤔 ambiguous, where the opposite answer could reasonably be argued: only the official answer is accepted, so memorize them. Finally, for English speakers, this page includes the full list of your vocabulary word and phrase look-ups. Again, you will need to review this vocabulary carefully, committing it to memory before you take the actual exam.

5. Weak spots and mock exams. Once you have cleared every level, the main page leads with two cards. 🎯 Weak spots holds every question you have missed in training or on a mock. Work through them between mocks, since they are exactly what the mocks keep catching. The drill goes topic by topic, in the same order the topics appear on the dashboard, with the hardest first inside each. It works in rounds: a round covers the whole list, so a question does not come back until every other one has been asked. A question leaves the list once you answer it correctly twice in a row, or after a single correct answer in Italiano when Italiano is not where you missed it, since answering under exam conditions proves more than a second answer at an easier level. A question you missed in Italiano still needs two. Any miss, while studying or on a mock, starts the run over. You can stop part way and come back: ▶ Resume returns you to the question you had reached, not to the top of the list, and it does so on any device you have synced.

The 📝 mock exam card starts 30 questions in 20 minutes in Italiano, and you pass with 3 wrong or fewer. Leave the mocks until you have cleared all three levels, since one taken earlier measures the Italian you have not yet learned rather than your knowledge of the rules. The card appears on the main page then, or earlier if you turn it on in ⚙️ Settings. The 📊 Statistics page keeps your results. Its ring gives your chance of failing the real exam, predicted from your mock exams, and the app tells you that you are ready once 15 mock exams put it at 5% or less. Below it are your past results and the questions you keep missing.

6. Reading the questions. These patterns decide the answer in every topic:

  • Watch the absolute words. Solo / soltanto (only), sempre (always), mai (never), tutti (all), and esclusivamente (exclusively) usually mark a statement stretched until it became false. Limitarsi a (to do nothing but) and basta (it is enough to) are "only" in disguise: "è necessario limitarsi ad osservare i limiti" claims the limits are all it takes.
  • The softeners work the other way. Di norma (normally), salvo diversa segnalazione (unless otherwise signed), and può (may) leave room for exceptions, so statements carrying them are usually TRUE. Watch può (may) against deve (must): swapping permission and duty is a favorite trick.
  • A true fact can carry a wrong condition. About one false statement in twenty is a correct rule, a comma, then an if, when, or provided that clause that applies it to the wrong case ("the stop line marks where to stop, if the junction is not controlled"). Read the condition after the comma as carefully as the rule before it.
  • Watch anche ("also / even"). It stretches a rule past where it holds: anche quando i distributori sono chiusi ("even when the pumps are closed") is exactly where a true rule turns false.
  • Answer the exact words, not the impression. Numbers, road types, and vehicle categories are swapped deliberately, and a statement that is almost right is wrong.
  • Answer what the law requires, not what a careful driver would do. Many statements dress cautious behavior up as a legal duty ("you must check the mirrors every time you go straight at an intersection"). If only prudence makes a statement true, the answer is usually FALSE.
  • Two verbs both come out as "pass" in English. Sorpassare (and the noun sorpasso) means only overtaking a vehicle. Superare means "to get past" whatever follows it: superare la striscia is crossing the line, superare il limite is exceeding the limit, and only with a vehicle as its object does it mean overtaking. So consente il sorpasso senza superarla means "allows overtaking without crossing the line."
  • An abstract noun with the definite article does not mean "the only cause". La pericolosità del sorpasso è determinata da… reads as "the danger of passing comes from…", so several parallel statements can all be TRUE.
  • A statement about a sign may have nothing to do with its purpose. "Il segnale consente X" is true even if X has nothing to do with the sign, unless the law forbids X. Conversely, "il segnale vieta X" or "obbliga a X" is false when X is unrelated to the sign.
  • The figure is part of the question. Many cards repeat the same sentences over a different image. Answer for exactly the sign or scene shown, not the one you remember from the previous card.
  • A few questions are genuinely ambiguous. Some official questions are worded so that the opposite answer could reasonably be argued. While you study they are marked with 🤔, and they are collected under 🤔 Ambiguous in 📗 Reference, so make sure you know the official answer to each.

How you get an Italian license — the full process

Getting a Category B license in Italy takes several stages, and this app prepares you for one of them, the theory exam. You can go through a driving school (autoscuola), which files your paperwork and books your exams, or apply as a private candidate (privatista) and do that yourself. Most immigrants pick a school and study the theory here instead of in the school's Italian class, since attending a theory course is not required.

Either way, you must be at least 18 and resident in Italy. A non-EU citizen must also show a valid residence permit (permesso di soggiorno).

  1. Study the theory. Work through the official questions in this app, in English and then in Italian, before anything else. It usually takes longer than 3 months, and the clocks in the later steps are short: the medical certificate is valid for 3 months, and the filed application gives you 6 months for the theory exam. Once you can answer all the questions correctly in Italian without help in English, you should be ready to book the two medical visits described in the steps below. Certainly, by the time the app suggests that you take mock exams, you should book them.
  2. Enroll. Choose an authorized driving school and sign up. The school tells you which documents and medical papers to bring, and it usually takes your photo for the application. There is no single directory of schools, because they are licensed locally, so search your area and confirm the school is authorized. A school can be an expensive choice of little value if it offers no support to a student whose Italian is limited, so shop around: ask whether the textbook comes in English as well as Italian, and whether an instructor speaks English well enough to answer your questions. Most of a school's clients are teenagers, and an older American may feel a little out of place in that setting, which is one more reason to visit before signing up.
  3. Get the medical-history note. Your own doctor (medico di base) writes the certificato anamnestico from the medical records they hold, usually without examining you. Your doctor charges for this note, because the national health service does not cover it. Expect to pay about €50. Read the note before you hand it in. Get it only when you are ready to file within 3 months, since the medical certificate it leads to must be under 3 months old when the application is filed.
    What the note asks. The note has one line for each of these conditions, in this order, and your doctor answers yes or no on each.
    • Heart and circulation (apparato cardio-circolatorio)
    • Diabetes (diabete mellito)
    • Endocrine system (sistema endocrino)
    • Nervous system (apparato neurologico)
    • Psychiatric conditions (patologie psichiche)
    • Epilepsy (epilessia)
    • Dependence on alcohol, drugs or psychoactive substances (condizioni di dipendenza da alcol/sostanze stupefacenti e psicotrope)
    • Urinary and genital system, severe kidney failure (apparato urogenitale, insufficienza renale grave)
    • Blood and blood-forming organs, meaning hemophilia, or a lymphoma or leukemia under treatment (sangue ed organi emopoietici)
    • Bones and joints, serious anatomical or functional impairment (apparato osteoarticolare)
    • Sense organs, serious eye disease that is getting worse (organi di senso)
    A yes in any of these categories can delay your license. The authorized doctor in the next step reviews every yes, may ask for a specialist's report first, and may send you to the local medical commission, where the wait for a visit can run to several months. If a line does not match your condition, ask your doctor about it before the note is used.
  4. Pass the medical. You book the visit with a Ministry-authorized doctor through the school, usually on its own premises. Bring the certificato anamnestico, since the doctor cannot issue the certificate without it. This doctor, not your own, checks your eyesight, reviews the note, and issues the medical certificate.
    If the note has a yes. This doctor decides whether to certify you or to send you to the local medical commission (Commissione Medica Locale). A yes does not by itself rule out a license, but expect it to take longer. The commission judges epilepsy unless a neurologist certifies it cured, past dependence on alcohol, regular use of medicines that affect driving, serious psychiatric and neurological conditions, kidney failure that needs dialysis or a transplant, and physical impairments. A heart condition needs a report from a cardiologist at a public facility, and diabetes a report from a diabetologist. With that report this doctor may certify you, or may send you to the commission. Any other doubt this doctor has about your fitness to drive also sends you there. For any condition marked on the note, bring a recent report from your specialist to this visit.
  5. The school files your application. It submits the TT2112 form to your provincial Motorizzazione office with your documents, the medical certificate and the fee receipts. The medical certificate must be under 3 months old when the application is filed. From then on you have 6 months and 2 attempts to pass the theory exam, and at least a month must pass between a failed attempt and the next.
  6. Pass the theory exam. It has 30 true/false questions in 20 minutes, and you may get no more than 3 wrong. It is offered in Italian, with French and German versions, and never in English. No interpreter or dictionary is allowed in the room. It is held in computerized rooms at the Motorizzazione, not at the school. This is what this app prepares you for.
    The school decides when to put you forward, and most will not do it until they have seen a good average on their own practice sheets, taken in the school's own app. Most schools use a publisher's textbook and practice app, and the school reads your averages there. Your work here is not visible to them, so plan on running their sheets as well once your study is done. This is the school's own condition and not a Ministry rule. Ask early what average it wants and over how many sheets, and how far ahead the exam dates are running.
    On the day. This is what candidates commonly meet, though offices differ. Your phone, switched off, goes into a locker with your coat and hat, and nothing comes into the room with you. A camera checks your face at the entrance and again at your desk, against the photo on your application. The staff explain the procedure in Italian only. Before the exam starts, the screen asks you to accept two statements, so ask your school beforehand what they say. The exam screen has the same layout as the mock exam in this app, and you can go back over your answers before ending it. The result may not appear on the screen at the end, and it can reach you later through the school.
  7. Get your foglio rosa and take the mandatory lessons. Passing the theory exam earns the foglio rosa, a learner permit valid 12 months. Before you may practice privately, you must complete 8 hours of certified lessons at the school, in four modules. They are 2 hours of vehicle handling and emergency drills, 3 hours of city driving, 2 hours on the autostrada or extra-urban roads, and 1 hour at night. After the school certifies them, you may practice with a companion who sits beside you, is no older than 65, and has held a B license for at least 10 years. The car must display a "P" plate.
  8. Pass the driving test. The school books it and provides the car, which must have dual controls. You may take it from 1 month after the foglio rosa, with up to 3 attempts within its 12 months and at least a month between a failed attempt and the next. A Motorizzazione examiner checks that you prepare the car for safe driving, watches two maneuvers, one of them in reverse, and rides with you for at least 25 minutes in normal traffic. Taking the test on an automatic is allowed, but the license then carries code 78 and is valid only for automatics.
  9. Receive your license. A B license covers cars and vans up to 3.5 t with up to 9 seats, a light trailer up to 750 kg, and, in Italy only, motorcycles up to 125 cc and 11 kW. It is valid 10 years until age 50, then 5 years to 70, 3 years to 80, and 2 years after that. As a new driver (neopatentato) you face extra limits for the first three years, such as reduced vehicle power and a stricter alcohol limit.

Cost. There is no single fee. The certificato anamnestico costs about €50, and the medical certificate carries a €16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) on top of the authorized doctor's fee. The school's enrollment and paperwork fee usually runs €150–300, more in big cities, and it normally includes the Ministry fees. Lessons are usually sold in 30-minute units of about €30 each, less in packages, so the 8 mandatory hours come to roughly €400–500. All in, getting the license through a school typically costs €800–1,500. Expect a small deposit when exams are booked, forfeited for an unjustified absence, and a 24-hour cancellation rule on lessons.

Add a copy of a valid ID, front and back, your codice fiscale or health card, front and back, the medical certificate, the pagoPA receipt and a 33 × 40 mm photo (some offices ask for two). A non-EU citizen also adds a copy of the residence permit. How you submit depends on the office, since some take it at the counter and others by email as a single PDF, so check yours first. The office then tells you the date of your theory exam. From filing you have 6 months and 2 attempts to pass it, and at least a month must pass between a failed attempt and the next.

Cost. This is the cheapest route and the most work. The certificato anamnestico costs about €50, and the authorized doctor charges a fee of their own, plus a €16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) on the certificate. The Ministry fees are €42.40 to enroll and €16 for the driving test. The 8 mandatory hours of lessons come to roughly €400–500, and the school charges for the use of its car at the test.

How long does it take? Usually 3–6 months start to finish. It can be as little as ~2 months if you study fast and exam slots are open, or up to ~18 months if you use the full legal windows (6 months for theory, a 12-month foglio rosa).

Already have a non-EU license (e.g. US)? It's valid in Italy only for your first 12 months of residency (carry an International Driving Permit or a sworn Italian translation). There's no conversion agreement with the US and no exemption for experience. You still take the full theory exam, practical exam, and mandatory lessons. Once the 12 months have passed, your foreign license no longer lets you drive in Italy at all. If you are stopped driving on it, you pay a fine and the police take the license on the spot and send it to the Motorizzazione (art. 135 of the Code). If that license has also expired, you are treated as driving without a license, a far heavier matter that includes the vehicle being impounded for three months. From then on you may drive only with a foglio rosa, only after the school has certified your mandatory lessons, and only with a qualified companion beside you. That is why many people start the process early in their first year.

Wherever you can, look for an English-speaking autoscuola or instructor, and start early in your 12-month window, while your foreign license still lets you drive.

Procedures vary by region and by provincial office, and they change over time. What is described here is the common case, not a rule for every office.
