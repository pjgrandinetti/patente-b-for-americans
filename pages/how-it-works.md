# How the app works

The Italian written test for a driver's license is given in Italian, and an American with a US license has to pass it like a first-time driver. So the app has two jobs, and it gives them equal weight. It teaches the road rules, and it teaches the Italian vocabulary and grammar needed to read the questions, since a misread word costs as many points as a rule never learned. Along the way, the learner's reading of Italian improves well beyond the test. This page describes each part of the app in the order a learner meets it. The background of the app is on the [About page](about).

## Every official question, in English and Italian

The Ministry publishes every true-or-false statement that can appear on the test, {LIVE} of them. The app has all of them, with the Ministry's Italian wording and answers unchanged. Each one carries an English translation and an explanation of the rule that decides the answer, in English and in Italian. When a statement is false, the explanation says what makes it false, and where a statement hangs on one word, such as *solo* (only) or *sempre* (always), it names the word.

By default the app runs in Abridged mode. It keeps every distinct idea and drops the near-duplicate wordings, which leaves about 4,350 statements. You can switch to the full bank in Settings.

## Three levels, from English to Italian only

You work through every topic three times, each time with less English.

At the first level you see each statement in Italian with its English translation. At the second you see the Italian alone, and you can tap an underlined word to see what it means; every word you tap goes into your own vocabulary list. At the third level, *Solo italiano*, there is no help at all, as in the real test. Each level keeps its own progress, and a card you clear at a harder level counts as cleared at the easier ones. If you already read Italian well, you can start at the third level.

## Learning the Italian, not only the answers

The questions are written in formal, legal Italian, so the app treats the language as part of the work.

At the second level, every word you tap goes into your own vocabulary list, and the list is there to review on the Reference page. When you miss a question at *Solo italiano*, the English of the statement and of its explanation appear under the Italian, with the words in that statement worth knowing and their meanings, including the rare ones you did not think to tap. The app also tells you when you answered a question right in English and missed it in Italian, because then the problem was the Italian, not the rule.

More than a thousand words carry a short note to help them stick. Words that look like English but mean something else, such as *pretendere* (to demand, not to pretend), carry a warning and a line on where the word comes from. Others name an English relative you may not have noticed, or break the word into its parts. A separate list gathers the Italian words that share one English meaning, and says which of them the rules keep apart, such as *fermata*, *sosta* and *arresto*.

The Grammar tab explains, for an English speaker, the constructions that make the questions hard to read. It shows how to take a long statement apart and find its main verb when the subject comes after it. It explains the formal linking words, such as *qualora* (if) and *purché* (provided that), who is doing what to whom in *sorpassa* and *sorpassato*, and the words that mark a duty, advice, a permission and a ban. Every example is an official question.

The Glossary defines, in Italian, the technical terms of the Highway Code, such as *carreggiata* and *banchina*, the way the law uses them. At *Solo italiano* those terms are underlined in the questions, and a tap shows the definition.

An added benefit is that, by the end, you will read formal Italian much more easily. Much of the test's vocabulary and grammar is the formal Italian of offices, forms and official notices, which you will meet long after the test.

## Study notes written for US drivers

Each of the 25 topics has its own study notes, the Concepts, with the signs pictured beside the rules. Where an Italian rule differs from what you know at home, a note for US drivers says so. Passing on the right is almost never allowed, low beams stay on in daylight outside town, the alcohol limit is about 0.05% against the 0.08% you know, and the license points count down from 20. Every question has a 📖 button that opens the notes at the paragraph that answers it. Every line of the notes has an IT button that shows the same line in Italian, so the notes become reading practice too.

## A reference page for words, grammar and numbers

The Reference page holds everything you would otherwise look up somewhere else. The Vocabulary, Grammar and Glossary tabs described above live there, under one search box, and a word you select in a question is carried into that box.

*Numbers* lists every speed, distance, mass, age and penalty the test asks about, and under each one, how many questions ask about it. You can hide the values and test yourself.

*Questions* searches the whole bank for a word or a question number.

## Weak spots, until they are gone

Every question you miss goes into your weak spots. Once you have cleared all three levels, the app takes you through them topic by topic, hardest first, and a question leaves the list once you answer it correctly twice in a row, or once at *Solo italiano* if you missed it at an easier level. A question you missed for the Italian is marked as such, and the first time round you can open its English before you answer.

## Mock exams that look like the real one

A mock exam is 30 questions in 20 minutes, in Italian only, and you pass with three mistakes or fewer, as in the real test. The screen copies the Ministry's exam screen, with the same layout, the same Italian buttons and the same summary page before you hand it in. Until then you can go back and change any answer. While the mock runs, the rest of the app is locked, so there is nothing to look up. Afterward you can review every question with its English and its explanation, and each mistake joins your weak spots.

## Your chance of failing, and when you are ready

The Statistics page predicts your chance of failing the real test from your mock exam scores, counting how much they vary as well as their average. A chart shows the range your next score is likely to fall in. The app tells you that you are ready once at least 15 mock exams put the chance of failing at 5% or less. The same page lists the questions you keep missing and the topics most worth reviewing.

## The weeks before your exam

Once you are ready and have a date, you enter it, and the app counts down to it. Each day it sets a review of the questions you have missed, sized to about an hour of work together with one mock exam. Time left over is best spent continuing the review, since a mock exam samples only thirty of the seven thousand statements while the review holds the questions you have actually missed. In the last week before the exam the box drops the hour and asks for as much of the review as you can manage, in two or three sittings a day, still with one mock exam a day. Once the day's set is done, you may continue with the questions still waiting if you have the time. The box shows the date, the advice for the days left, and three rows for today, the review, the questions still waiting and the mock exam. A closed line under it, What is waiting in the review, states the three-day rule and counts the waiting questions by how many correct days each still needs and gives the minimum number of correct answers before the review is empty. It also links to the Reference page's Numbers tab filtered to the figures consistently missed, the figures with two or more questions you have missed in Italian and not yet answered correctly on three different days, each with its count, so the numbers to study in the last week are one tap away. A question leaves the review only when you have answered it correctly on three different days, since what you can answer on several different days is what you are likely to remember on the day of the test.

## Free, private, and on all your devices

The app is free, with no ads, no account and no cookies. It works offline, and on an iPhone you can add it to the home screen like any other app. If you study on more than one device, a sync code keeps them in step, and your progress is encrypted before it leaves the device. A 🔊 button reads any statement aloud in Italian.

The app also covers the paperwork. Its How-to describes every step from the doctor's visits to the driving test, for the driving-school route and for a private candidate, and the same steps are on [their own page](process).
