# The driving test, explained (draft, 2026-10-29)

Draft of the stage 7 content for a learner holding the foglio rosa. English
face only; the Italian face follows once the English is agreed. Nothing here
is in the app yet.

**Source.** Every rule below comes from the Motorizzazione's circular to
examiners, prot. 28819 of 19 September 2019, "Conseguimento patente B"
(text at patente.it/normativa/circolare-19-09-2019-n-28819-conseguimento-patente-b),
which replaced circ. 21284 of 28 September 2016. The one section that does not
come from it is marked as such. Two of its figures are out of date and are NOT
used: it says the foglio rosa lasts six months and allows one retry; the
How-to's current rules (12 months, up to three attempts, a month between
attempts) stand.

## How the test works

The test has three parts, taken in order, and you must pass each part to go on
to the next. The examiner sits in the car with you, and your instructor sits
beside you with dual controls. The office allows about 40 minutes for each
candidate, of which 25 are driving in traffic.

You may take the test in a car with a manual or an automatic gearbox. A test
passed in an automatic puts code 78 on your license, and that license does not
let you drive a manual.

## Part 1: getting ready to drive

This part is not a theory test. The examiner watches you prepare the car the
way a careful driver does.

- Adjust the seat for distance from the pedals and the wheel, then its height and the angle of the backrest.
- Adjust the interior mirror and both side mirrors.
- Fasten your seat belt, and set its height if the car allows it.
- Set the head restraint.
- Check that the doors and the trunk are closed, and know where the open-door warning light is.
- Know how to open the hood and how the child locks work.
- Be ready to check or use at least two items the examiner picks at random. They are the tires, the steering, the brakes, the horn and lights, and the fluid levels (oil, coolant, brake fluid and washer fluid).
- Be ready to switch on the low beams, high beams, turn signals, hazard lights and fog lights, and to sound the horn.

## Part 2: the maneuvers

You perform at least two of the four maneuvers below, and at least one of them
is in reverse. The examiner says which ones and how. If you make a mistake in a
maneuver, the examiner may let you repeat it. A second mistake in the same
maneuver ends the test.

### Reversing

You may be asked to reverse in a straight line or around a corner.

- Look in the interior mirror and the right mirror.
- Switch on the right turn signal.
- Turn your head and upper body to check the blind spot on the right directly. The examiner must see you do it.
- Reverse slowly, parallel to the curb, with small corrections of the wheel. Short glances at the mirrors are allowed.
- Around a corner, keep both hands on the wheel and rely more on the mirrors.

### Turning around

You turn the car to face the other way, using forward and reverse gears. You
must do it in several moves, even if the road is wide enough to turn in one.
The examiner tells you this before you start.

- Start at the right edge of the road, in first gear.
- Look ahead, behind and to both sides.
- Switch on the left turn signal, then move forward slowly while turning the wheel.
- Stop close to the left edge without touching the curb.
- Turn the wheel the other way, switch on the right turn signal, and reverse.
- Select first gear, switch on the left turn signal, check the traffic, and pull out into the right lane.

### Parking

You may be asked to park parallel to the curb, in a bay at a right angle, or in
a bay at an angle, forward or in reverse, on level ground or on a slope. Then
you pull out again.

- Before you start, check behind and to both sides.
- The exact distance from the curb does not matter. What matters is that you touch nothing, put nobody at risk, block no traffic, and finish inside the bay.
- To pull out, signal, check every mirror and both blind spots, then move off promptly and join the right lane.

### Stopping at a given point

The examiner names a point, and you bring the car to a stop exactly there. An
emergency stop may also be asked for, but it is optional.

## Part 3: driving in traffic

You drive for 25 minutes on roads the office chooses, in ordinary traffic, and
follow the examiner's directions. The drive may include any of the following.

- Moving off from a parking space, from a stop in traffic, or out of a side road.
- Straight roads, including passing oncoming traffic where the road is narrow.
- Bends.
- Junctions of every kind.
- Turning left and right, and changing lanes.
- Joining and leaving the autostrada by its ramps. On the autostrada you may use only the two lanes nearest the right edge.
- Passing other vehicles and obstacles, and being passed.
- Roundabouts, level crossings, bus and tram stops, pedestrian crossings, long hills and tunnels.
- Getting out of the car safely at the end.

### Roundabouts

The circular gives examiners exact rules for roundabouts, because they are where
candidates most often go wrong.

- A roundabout is a junction, and each exit is a turn.
- Approach slowly and be ready to yield. Nearly every roundabout has a yield sign at its entrances, which gives priority to the cars already inside. Where there is no sign, the car entering has priority, by the rule of yielding to the right.
- Never signal left as you enter a single-lane roundabout.
- To leave by the first exit, signal right as you approach. For a later exit, signal right just after you pass the exit before yours.
- On a roundabout with two or more lanes, the lane you use depends on your exit:
  - to exit right, approach near the right edge and keep to the right lane;
  - to go straight in light traffic, keep to the right lane and signal right just before your exit;
  - to go straight in heavy traffic, enter in a free lane and keep it, then move to the right lane with a signal before your exit;
  - to exit left, approach in the left lane as for a left turn, signal left as you enter, and keep to the left lane. Before your exit, signal right and move to the right lane without cutting anyone off. If heavy traffic keeps you out of the left lane, stay in the right lane all the way round.

## What the examiner judges

The examiner judges partly by whether you obey the signs and the rules, and
partly by their own judgment of how safely you drive. The circular names these
areas.

- **Control of the car.** You use the belt, mirrors, seat, lights, clutch, gears, accelerator, brakes and steering correctly, at every speed and in every situation.
- **Observation.** You look all around the car, use the mirrors, and look near, middle and far ahead.
- **Priority.** You yield where you must, at junctions, when reversing, when changing lanes and at pedestrian crossings.
- **Position.** You keep the right lane on a straight road, at a roundabout and in a bend.
- **Distance.** You keep a safe distance from the vehicle ahead, from vehicles beside you and from other road users.
- **Speed.** You keep to the limit, adapt to the traffic and the weather, and can always stop within the distance you can see is clear.
- **Signs and markings.** You obey traffic lights, police officers, signs and road markings.
- **Signaling.** You signal in good time, switch the signal off afterward, and respond to other drivers' signals.
- **Braking and stopping.** You brake early and smoothly, and take care when you park and get out.

The examiner also judges your prudence and your care for others, above all for
pedestrians and cyclists. The circular tells the examiner to allow for nerves,
but not at the cost of safety.

## What ends the test at once

Any mistake that puts someone in danger fails the test, whether or not the
instructor had to step in. In particular, the test ends at once for any of the
following.

- The instructor uses the dual controls or touches the car's controls to avoid a danger.
- A second mistake in the same maneuver.
- Failing to yield at a junction, or pulling out of a side road without yielding.
- Going through a red light, or against a police officer's signal.
- Going more than 40 km/h over the limit.
- Driving on the wrong side of the road in a bend, over a crest, where you cannot see far enough, or on a divided road.
- Passing in a bend, over a crest, where you cannot see, at a junction, at a level crossing, or a vehicle that is itself passing.
- Passing badly, that is, too slowly, too close to the side, or without moving back to the right as soon as you can.
- Reversing or driving the wrong way on the autostrada, or misusing its shoulder or its ramp lanes.
- Not taking the greatest care at a level crossing.
- Entering a junction you cannot clear.
- A collision caused by following too closely.
- Driving without your seat belt, or using a phone.

The result is given at the end of the test, with a reason if you fail. If you
pass, you receive your license there and then.

## Habits worth checking (not from the circular)

These are rules of the Code that an experienced American driver may break out
of habit. Each one is something the examiner judges under the areas above.

- At a STOP sign, the wheels must stop completely at the line, even when nothing is coming.
- There is no right turn on red. You wait unless a green arrow allows the turn.
- At a yellow light, stop if you can do so safely.
- At a zebra crossing, stop for a pedestrian who is crossing or about to cross.
- At a junction with no signs, a vehicle coming from your right has priority.
- Never pass on the right.
- Make every check visible. The examiner can judge only what they see you do.
