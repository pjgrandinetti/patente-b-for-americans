Getting a Category B license in Italy involves several stages, and this app prepares you for one of them, the theory exam. You can go through a driving school (autoscuola), which files your paperwork and schedules your exams, or apply as a private candidate (privatista) and handle it yourself. Most immigrants enroll in a school for the paperwork and study the theory in this app, since the school's theory course is not required.

Either way, you must be at least 18 years old and a resident of Italy. A non-EU citizen must also present a valid residence permit (permesso di soggiorno).

- Study the theory first. Work through the official questions in this app, first in English and then in Italian. This usually takes more than 3 months. Some of the later steps have deadlines once they begin (step 3 explains them), so study the theory first. Once you are making good progress, you can start the steps below.

- Decide between a driving school and the private route. The two routes differ significantly in cost. The Cost paragraph for each route, below, provides the figures. Before you decide, visit two or three driving schools. They are licensed locally, and there is no single directory, so search your area.

If you are considering enrolling in a school, ask the following:

- What does enrollment include? Ask for the price, item by item. The items include the paperwork and the Motorizzazione fees, the visit to the Ministry-authorized doctor, the theory course, the school's mock exam app, the mandatory driving lessons, and the car for the driving test.

- May you study the theory on your own instead of attending the course? Schools usually track your progress in their own app and decide, based on it, when to put you forward for the theory exam. Ask whether your results in this app will be accepted as proof that you are ready. The Statistics page of this app shows your mock exams and your chance of failing. If not, ask what score on the school's own mock exams it requires first.

- Which publisher's textbook and software does the school use, and can it provide the textbook in English? Many schools work with SIDA, whose manual is available in an English edition.

- Does an instructor speak English well enough to answer your questions? A school with no language support can be an expensive choice with little value. Even with language support, keep in mind that a driving school teaches the rules, not the language. Its theory course is not meant to help you with Italian grammar and vocabulary, so it may be of little use if Italian is what you struggle with.

If you are considering the private route, you will still need a driving school once you have the foglio rosa. The mandatory driving lessons, 6 hours behind the wheel with an instructor, must be completed at a school. The official driving test is almost always taken in a school's dual-control car. Ask:

- Does the school offer the mandatory driving lessons to a private candidate? How much does a driving lesson cost, and how many hours does it cover? What is the price for the 6 mandatory hours? If you have driven for years, the 6 hours may be all you need. If you have driven only automatics and want a license without code 78, plan on more, so the price of one lesson matters.

- How soon can the driving lessons be scheduled once you have your foglio rosa?

- Can the driving lessons and the driving test be taken in an automatic if that is what you drive? The license then carries code 78.

- Does the school provide its car for a private candidate's official driving test, and what does it charge for its use?

- Get the certificato anamnestico (medical-history note). This is a certificate documenting your medical history. Your own doctor (medico di base) prepares it from their records, usually without examining you. The certificato anamnestico includes one line for each condition listed below, and your medico di base marks each line yes or no. The national health service does not cover the certificato anamnestico, so your medico di base will charge for it. Expect to pay about €50.

When to get it. The certificato anamnestico is valid for 3 months and must still be valid when you file your application for the theory exam. The application then gives you 6 months to take the exam, with at most two attempts. So get the certificato anamnestico when you expect to pass the theory exam within about 6 months. For most learners, that is when they have mastered the questions in English and are working through them in Italian. Then take the next steps in your route in the following weeks, and bring the certificato anamnestico with you.

What the certificato anamnestico asks about.

- Heart and circulation (apparato cardio-circolatorio)

- Diabetes (diabete mellito)

- Endocrine system (sistema endocrino)

- Nervous system (apparato neurologico)

- Psychiatric conditions (patologie psichiche)

- Epilepsy (epilessia)

- Dependence on alcohol, drugs or psychoactive substances (condizioni di dipendenza da alcol/sostanze stupefacenti e psicotrope)

- Urinary and genital system, severe kidney failure (apparato urogenitale, insufficienza renale grave)

- Blood and blood-forming organs, meaning hemophilia, or a lymphoma or leukemia under treatment (sangue ed organi emopoietici)

- Bones and joints, serious anatomical or functional impairment (apparato osteoarticolare)

- Sense organs, serious eye disease that is getting worse (organi di senso)

Avoid a yes if you can. When you receive the certificato anamnestico, review the answers for each condition before you leave your medico di base's office. A yes for any of these conditions could delay your license by months if, when you apply for the theory exam, the Ministry-authorized doctor (medico autorizzato) reads the certificato anamnestico and decides that your case must be reviewed by the local medical commission, where appointments are often several months out. Any condition that is cured, under control, or mild may not need to be marked as yes. If you disagree with your medico di base on any of these answers, you may need to visit a specialist and get their opinion. If the specialist agrees with you, bring their note back to the medico di base and ask for an updated certificato anamnestico. That takes time, but it will be far less than if the Ministry-authorized doctor (medico autorizzato) sends you to the local medical commission.

## Through a driving school

## As a private candidate

- Enroll. Choose an authorized driving school and sign up, bringing the certificato anamnestico from step 3. The school will tell you which other documents to bring, and it usually takes your photo for the application. Choose it with the questions in step 2.

- Visit the Ministry-authorized doctor. The school books your visit with a Ministry-authorized doctor (medico autorizzato), usually on its own premises. Bring the certificato anamnestico. The doctor checks your eyesight, reviews the certificato anamnestico, and issues the medical certificate (certificato medico) for your application. If the certificato anamnestico has a yes, the doctor will likely send your case to the local medical commission (Commissione Medica Locale), as step 3 explains. If you could not get the yes removed, bring a recent report from your specialist to the visit. The commission will want it, and the rules allow the doctor to certify certain conditions based on such a report, though practice varies from one doctor to the next.

- The school files your application. It submits the TT2112 form to your provincial Motorizzazione office along with your documents, the medical certificate, and the fee receipts. The medical certificate must be no more than 3 months old when the application is filed. From the filing date, you have 6 months and 2 attempts to pass the theory exam. At least a month must pass between a failed attempt and the next.

Pass the theory exam. It has 30 true/false questions to be completed in 20 minutes, and you may get no more than 3 wrong. It is offered in Italian, with French and German versions, and never in English. No interpreter or dictionary is allowed in the room. It is held in computerized rooms at the Motorizzazione, not at the school. This app prepares you for this exam.

The school decides when to book your theory exam. Most schools wait until you have a good average on the school's mock exams in its own app, and your work in this app is not visible to the school. So plan to take the school's mock exams as well once your study is done, unless the school accepts your results here (the question in step 2). This is the school's own condition, not a Ministry rule. Ask early what average it wants, over how many mock exams, and how far ahead the exam dates are booked.

On the day. This is what candidates commonly encounter, though offices vary. Your phone, switched off, goes into a locker with your coat and hat. Nothing comes into the room with you. A camera checks your face at the entrance and again at your desk, matching the photo on your application. The staff explains the procedure only in Italian. Before the exam starts, the screen asks you to accept two statements, so ask your school beforehand what they say. You must accept them to begin. The exam screen has the same layout as the mock exam in this app, and you can review your answers before ending it. The result may not appear on the screen at the end, and it can reach you later through the school.

- Get your foglio rosa and take the mandatory driving lessons. Passing the theory exam earns the foglio rosa, a learner permit valid for 12 months. You must complete 6 hours of certified driving lessons at the school. The 6 hours are divided into three 2-hour blocks. One block is on the autostrada or a main extra-urban road, one on a secondary extra-urban road or an urban arterial road, and one at night. You may take at most 2 hours of driving lessons per day. The driving test can be booked only after all 6 hours are certified. A November 2025 decree raises the requirement to 8 hours, in four modules, for a foglio rosa issued after the decree takes effect, so ask your school which rule applies to you. After the school certifies the driving lessons, you may practice with a companion who sits beside you. The companion must be no older than 65 and must have held a B license, or a higher category, for at least 10 years. The car must display a "P" plate.

- Pass the driving test. The school books the test and provides the car, which must have dual controls. You may take the test from 1 month after the foglio rosa is issued. You have up to 3 attempts within its 12-month validity, with at least 1 month between a failed attempt and the next. The test may be held in a different town from the theory exam, one of several towns in the province, so ask the school where yours will be. A candidate from outside the EU must show a valid residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) or the receipt for its application or renewal at the test. A Motorizzazione examiner checks that you prepare the car for safe driving, observes two maneuvers, one in reverse, and rides with you for at least 25 minutes in normal traffic. You may take the test in an automatic, but the license then carries code 78 and is valid only for automatics.

- Receive your license. A B license covers cars and vans up to 3.5 t with up to 9 seats, a light trailer up to 750 kg, and, in Italy only, motorcycles up to 125 cc and 11 kW. It is valid for 10 years until age 50, then 5 years to 70, 3 years to 80, and 2 years after that. As a new driver (neopatentato), you have additional restrictions for the first three years, such as a cap on vehicle power and a stricter alcohol limit.

Cost. There is no single fee. The certificato anamnestico costs about €50, and the medical certificate carries a €16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) on top of the Ministry-authorized doctor's fee. The school's enrollment and paperwork fee usually runs €150 to €300, more in big cities, and it normally includes the Ministry fees. Driving lessons are usually sold in 30-minute units of about €30 each, with packages priced lower. The 6 mandatory hours therefore come to roughly €300 to €400. All in, getting the license through a school typically costs €800 to €1,500. When an exam is booked, the school usually asks for a small deposit, which you lose if you miss the exam without a valid reason. A driving lesson usually has to be canceled 24 hours in advance.

- Visit a Ministry-authorized doctor. You book the visit yourself with a Ministry-authorized doctor (medico autorizzato), either at the ASL medical office through the CUP or with a doctor who sees candidates at a driving school or at a vehicle-paperwork agency (agenzia di pratiche auto). Bring the certificato anamnestico. The doctor checks your eyesight, reviews the certificato anamnestico, and issues the medical certificate (certificato medico) for your application. The medical certificate must be less than 3 months old when you file your application. If the certificato anamnestico has a yes, the doctor will likely send your case to the local medical commission (Commissione Medica Locale), as step 3 explains. If you could not get the yes removed, bring a recent report from your specialist to the visit. The commission will want it, and the rules allow the doctor to certify certain conditions based on such a report, though practice varies from one doctor to the next.

- Pay the fees. Pay via pagoPA through the Portale dell'Automobilista in your own name. The application is tariff N067, €26.40 plus €16 stamp duty, €42.40 in total. Keep the receipt with its IUV code. The driving test later requires tariff N019, €16.

- File your application. Complete and sign the TT2112 form, then submit it to your provincial Motorizzazione office. Include copies of a valid ID (front and back), your codice fiscale or health card (front and back), the medical certificate, the pagoPA receipt, and a 33 × 40 mm photo (some offices ask for two). Non-EU citizens should also include a copy of the residence permit. Some offices accept applications at the counter, while others accept them by email as a single PDF, so ask your office first. The office will then provide the date of your theory exam. From the day of filing, you have 6 months and 2 attempts to pass it. At least a month must pass between a failed attempt and the next.

Pass the theory exam. It has 30 true/false questions to be completed in 20 minutes, and you may get no more than 3 wrong. It is offered in Italian, with French and German versions, and never in English. No interpreter or dictionary is allowed in the room. It is held in computerized rooms at the Motorizzazione. This app prepares you for this exam.

On the day. This is what candidates commonly encounter, though procedures vary by office. Your phone, switched off, goes into a locker with your coat and hat. Nothing comes into the room with you. A camera checks your face at the entrance and again at your desk, matching the photo on your application. The staff explains the procedure only in Italian. Before the exam starts, the screen asks you to accept two statements, so find out beforehand what they say. You must accept them to begin. The exam screen has the same layout as the mock exam in this app, and you can review your answers before ending it. The result may not appear on the screen at the end, but you can receive it later.

- Get your foglio rosa and take the mandatory driving lessons. Passing the theory exam earns the foglio rosa, a learner permit valid for 12 months. Private candidates need a driving school. The 6 hours of mandatory driving lessons must be taken at an authorized school, in the three blocks listed for the school route. The driving test cannot be booked until the school certifies the 6 hours. Choose a school now, using the questions in step 2. You will most likely take the driving test in the school's car too (next step). After the school certifies the driving lessons, you may practice with a companion who sits beside you. The companion must be no older than 65 and must have held a B license, or a higher category, for at least 10 years. The car must display a "P" plate.

- Pass the driving test. The test car must have dual controls, so in practice you take the test in a driving school's car. Some offices require a private candidate to go through a school for this step. You may take the test from 1 month after the foglio rosa is issued. You have up to 3 attempts within its 12-month validity, with at least a month between a failed attempt and the next. It may be held in a different town from the theory exam, one of several towns in the province, so ask the school where yours will be. A candidate from outside the EU must show a valid residence permit (permesso di soggiorno) or the receipt for its application or renewal at the test. Bring the same TT2112 file, now with your foglio rosa, your driving-lesson record, and the N019 receipt. The test itself is the same as on the school route.

- Receive your license. A B license covers cars and vans up to 3.5 t with up to 9 seats, a light trailer up to 750 kg, and, in Italy only, motorcycles up to 125 cc and 11 kW. It is valid for 10 years until age 50, then 5 years to 70, 3 years to 80, and 2 years after that. As a new driver (neopatentato), you have additional restrictions for the first three years, such as a cap on vehicle power and a stricter alcohol limit.

Cost. This is the cheapest route, but it requires the most work. The certificato anamnestico costs about €50, and the Ministry-authorized doctor charges a fee plus a €16 revenue stamp (marca da bollo) on the medical certificate. The Ministry fees are €42.40 for the application and €16 for the driving test. The 6 mandatory hours of driving lessons cost roughly €300 to €400. The school also charges for the use of its car during the driving test.

How long does it take? Usually 3 to 6 months from start to finish. It can be as little as about 2 months if you study quickly and exam dates are available. It can run to about 18 months if you use the full legal periods, 6 months for the theory exam and 12 months for the foglio rosa.

Already have a non-EU license (e.g., US)? It is valid in Italy only for your first 12 months of residency, and you must carry an International Driving Permit or a sworn Italian translation. There is no conversion agreement with the US and no exemption for experience. You still take the full theory exam, the driving test, and the mandatory driving lessons. Once the 12 months have passed, your foreign license no longer lets you drive in Italy at all. If you are stopped while driving on it, you pay a fine, and the police take the license on the spot and send it to the Motorizzazione (art. 135 of the Code). If that license has also expired, you are treated as driving without a license, a far heavier offense, and the vehicle is impounded for three months. After the 12 months, you may drive only with a foglio rosa, after a school has certified your mandatory driving lessons, and only with a qualified companion beside you. That is why many people start the process early in their first year.

Whenever possible, look for an English-speaking autoscuola or instructor, and start early during your first 12 months, while your foreign license still allows you to drive.

Procedures vary by region and by provincial office, and they change over time. What is described here is the common case, not a rule for every office.
